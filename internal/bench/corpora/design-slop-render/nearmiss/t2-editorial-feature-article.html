The last ferry keepers

Three crossings a day, a crew of four and a timetable that has not changed since the bridge opened. A season aboard the river's final passenger ferry.

Words by the features desk · Photographs by the picture desk · 14 min read

The first crossing leaves at ten past six, before the fish market has finished unloading. On a weekday in October it carries eleven people: two nurses coming off night shift, a cluster of schoolchildren, and a man who has made the same journey with the same bicycle for thirty-one years.

The crew know most passengers by the bags they carry. The ticket machine was removed a decade ago; fares are paid in coins dropped into a steel box bolted beside the wheelhouse door, and nobody has ever counted them in front of anyone else.

"When the bridge opened, people said we'd last a year. That was the year my daughter was born."

The council's transport review, due in spring, will decide whether the service survives another winter. The crew have read the draft. They have opinions about page fourteen.
