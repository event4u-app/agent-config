## Round 1 — three omitted duties

1. **The mid-flight interrupt protocol.** The law's `NEW TASK → FRESH
   CONFIRMATION` could be read as confirming *before* starting the new task.
   The body requires STOP → run the new task in full → ASK before resuming,
   and states that autonomy never authorizes a silent resume.
2. **The commit default** — "never commit, never ask" — which the rule's own
   opening sentence lists as one of the four things it defines.
3. **The allowlist trigger**: past 20 entries in one session, stop expanding
   and propose redesign or removal.

All three were repaired before round 2.

## Round 2 — owner, and a CONTRADICTION rather than an omission

Both seats found further conditional duties, and one found something worse
than an omission in the text I had written:

> the law said `THE VALIDATION-LOOP BOUND TRIGGERS A STRATEGY CHANGE, NEVER A
> QUESTION`, while § Validation-loop budget explicitly permits `THE RESIDUE
> OWNER-OWNED → ONE NATIVE ASK`.

An omission withholds a duty; a contradiction invents one. A thinned consumer
would have been told "never" where the rule says "sometimes".

A second, smaller drift: the opening claims the rule "defines" the commit
default, while the body asserts it and delegates the policy to `commit-policy`
— "anchors" or "restates" would be accurate.
