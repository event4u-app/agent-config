## Verdict

**owner** — AI council 2026-10-10, two rounds, anthropic + openai, 2/2 both
rounds, $0.00 metered. **The heading did NOT land and has been reverted;
`question-not-instruction` stays in the `missing` baseline.**

## Round 1 — refuse, on a section that changed not one character of the law

This is the instructive one. Round 1's change added **only** a `## The Iron
Law` heading above a fence that was already in the file. No word of the law
was altered. It was still refused, and correctly:

> the fence lists `"KANNST DU…?"` as a question signal and says NEVER EXECUTE
> ON A QUESTION, while the rule's **When it does NOT fire** section permits
> *"kannst du bitte X machen"* where context makes it a directive.

The contradiction had been in the file all along. The heading is what makes
that text the standalone delivery, so promoting it is what makes a latent
inconsistency load-bearing. **"No text change" does not imply "no obligation
change"** once the heading decides what ships alone — which is precisely the
question step 4.2 asks, and the author's prediction that this rule would pass
trivially was wrong for exactly this reason.

## Round 2 — another omitted conditional

The repair imported the body's polite-imperative carve-out into the fence.
Round 2 found a further one: the body permits execution when a rhetorical
question carries a trailing imperative ("why not X? do it"), so a thinned
consumer could reject `Why not use X? Do it.` while a full consumer executes
it — an authorization mismatch across a trust boundary.

## A widening the review was asked to judge, not told about afterwards

Round 2's own question flagged that the repair edited the **existing fenced
law**, not just the heading, and therefore changed the law for every consumer
rather than only thinned ones. The seats were asked explicitly whether that
falls under "a law section written from blocks the rule already states" or
needs its own separate change. Raising it in the question rather than the
record is what keeps a scope expansion from being booked as a repair.

## What the owner is being asked

Two things, and they are separable:

1. Whether this rule should carry a standing law, given that two conditional
   carve-outs resisted it across two rounds.
2. Whether the fence's current wording — unchanged, still listing
   `"KANNST DU…?"` while the body carves it out — should be corrected on its
   own merits, independently of any thinning. That contradiction exists today
   for every consumer who reads the rule whole; the review only made it
   visible.
